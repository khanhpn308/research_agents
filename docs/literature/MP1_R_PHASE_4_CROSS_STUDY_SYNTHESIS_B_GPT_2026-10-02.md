## 5. Theme-by-theme synthesis: seven axes within five themes

Five themes are sufficient: T01 combines pressure-controlled stiffness and the local-force chain; T02 combines slip mechanics and interface attribution; T03 covers structural TiNi; T04 separates actuation from structure and tests the decomposition logic; T05 integrates competing explanations. Each theme cites, compares, contrasts, connects and critiques evidence. This is analytical synthesis, not Chapter 2 prose.


### T01 — Pressure paths, confinement and the missing local-force map

Evidence boundary: S01, S02, S03, S15, S18, S22; MODERATE; MECHANISTIC_ANALOGUE; P401, P402, P403, P408, P416, P417. Phase 3 lineage: C001, C002, C003, C004, C014, C023, C024. Axes: 1, 5.

**Established knowledge.** S02 and S03 establish positive-pressure jamming in granular and nylon-fiber structures, while S01 supplies vacuum fiber/rope tunability and S18 a vacuum PVC layer-beam continuum model. This is strong evidence for the existence of architecture-dependent confinement effects (P401/P402), rather than for a universal pressure-to-stiffness relation. S15 frames laminar mechanisms only; its review excludes fiber and particle jamming and provides no independent experimental replication.

**Comparison across studies.** S02 expands a bladder against particles inside a fabric sleeve; S03 expands a bladder against nylon fibers bounded by rigid links. Both apply positive pressure, but particles and long fibers transmit load through different contact networks and boundaries. S18 instead compresses flat layers under vacuum through a flexible membrane and validates a continuum/slip deformation prediction in a cantilever. S03 uses segmented three-point-bending force curves, so even the measured structural observables cannot be pooled with S18 without boundary and metric conversion.

**Important contrasts.** S03 finds pressure-sensitive transition loads and slipping-branch stiffness alongside a relatively geometry-dominated jammed branch. Thus an endpoint stiffness increase does not imply that every branch has the same pressure dependence. Vacuum versus positive pressure is a boundary-path contrast, not a contradiction. S18 experimental vacuum confinement is 60 kPa; its separate 0.1 MPa FE loading is not a measured pressure sweep. S18 is a theoretical analogue to S03, not a positive-pressure fiber precursor.

**Mechanistic connections.** CROSS-STUDY INFERENCE: the sources connect pressure inputs to architecture-dependent confinement and then to modeled resistance/sliding constraints. This connection can motivate an MP1-R pressure-conditioned structural envelope and conventional baselines. It cannot close the intermediate pressure-to-contact map: S03 assumes effective uniform confinement, S18 assumes the threshold μp, and S22 infers normal force from resistance using a borrowed coefficient.

**Methodological critique.** The rigid reaction wall in S03 makes outward bladder expansion mechanically meaningful; a compliant or insufficiently constrained MP1-R sleeve may instead expand, rearrange packing, or change sleeve bending resistance. S18 omits transverse normal stress and admits slip-region/end/large-slip errors. Global model agreement can therefore coexist with incorrect local contact assignment. Treating p as every interface normal force would hide exactly the geometry-dependent mechanism that MP1-R needs to characterize.

**MP1-R relevance.** The pressure-controlled tunability principle is established prior art. MP1-R can investigate its actual reaction path and conditional bending response, with source pressure, specimen pressure and local contact loading represented as distinct quantities. No source supplies transferable nylon/PVC friction or modulus values for TiNi.

**Remaining uncertainty.** The actual fluid, chamber, sleeve compliance, packing and reaction surface are still unspecified. The effect of outward loading on wire–wire and wire–sleeve contacts may differ in sign or magnitude. L04 is the weakest causal link, and pressure-conditioned bending measurements alone will not resolve it.


### T02 — Slip-conditioned bending and interface identifiability

Evidence boundary: S03, S04, S06, S11, S18, S22, V-W02; ANALOGUE_ONLY; MECHANISTIC_ANALOGUE; P403, P404, P405, P408, P409, P411, P413, P418. Phase 3 lineage: C008, C009, C011, C013, C014, C021, C023, C024. Axes: 2, 6.

**Established knowledge.** S06 validates useful steel-rope stiffness limits associated with prescribed stick/slip kinematics. S18 represents evolving jammed/sliding regions in vacuum layers, while S03 represents pressure-conditioned fiber branches through an effective-section model. S04 directly measures wire–rubber sliding resistance; S22 directly measures selected internal cable-layer resistance. These results establish different modeling and measurement capabilities, not a single complete local contact law.

**Comparison across studies.** S06 and S11 both examine rope bending with attention to tension suppression, making stiffness-bound comparisons conditional on rope construction and fixture. Their targets differ: S06 compares cycle slopes with analytical limits; S11 characterizes/fits pinched loops and tests absorber performance. S18 adds cross-sectional sliding-region evolution, but for flat PVC layers. V-W02 adds transformation-aware frictional cable FE under axial load. These are complementary H0b ingredients whose domains must remain visible.

**Important contrasts.** Existence of friction is weaker than identifying local N, and neither is equivalent to identifying a dominant interface. S04 pull-out selects a wire–rubber path; S22 selectively cut cable layers select internal helical interfaces. They do not constitute a joint wire–wire/wire–sleeve partition. S06 does not implement the calibrated Coulomb/contact-pressure or cycle-evolution law sometimes attributed to it. S03 branch segmentation and S18 modeled regions do not directly image individual contacts slipping.

**Mechanistic connections.** CROSS-STUDY INFERENCE: slip constraints alter effective load sharing and can change bending slopes, while sliding offers a conventional route to dissipation. S06 constrains attainable limits, S18/S03 offer distinct transition representations, and S04/S22 show how independent interface observables can constrain a global response fit. The combination is a reason to test conventional models before adding new constitutive terms.

**Methodological critique.** An interface force test observes resistance approximately proportional to μN plus fixture and end losses. S22 derives N from a borrowed μ and approximate area; it therefore cannot independently validate μ, contact area and normal-force transfer. Dissection/pull-out changes packing and boundary conditions, so method existence does not guarantee valid parameters for intact bending. Likewise, fitting two unknown interface resistances to one unconstrained bending curve supplies parameters but not necessarily unique attribution.

**MP1-R relevance.** G1 remains a system-identification and experimental-design requirement. Global bending may identify an empirical envelope, but dominant-interface claims require independent constraints, selectively altered interfaces, or validated local slip/resistance observations. Pull-out is an available method, not a uniquely necessary fixture or automatic scientific contribution.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Neither global bending nor the currently validated analogue tests partitions internal and external losses in the proposed TiNi assembly. Quantitative slip progression, curvature dependence and complete hysteresis prediction remain model-specific and untested for MP1-R.


### T03 — Structural TiNi state, hysteresis and conventional transformation-aware models

Evidence boundary: S05, S07, S11, S12, S13, V-W02; ANALOGUE_ONLY; MECHANISTIC_ANALOGUE; P406, P407, P412, P419. Phase 3 lineage: C007, C010, C011, C012, C019. Axes: 3.

**Established knowledge.** S05 cable/component tension with DIC and IR provides direct evidence that local NiTi transformation changes structural response in its tested domain. S13 thermal/DMA comparisons demonstrate temperature- and braid-dependent response, and S11 observes mixed-rope hysteresis and absorber behavior. S12 and V-W02 establish the existence of transformation-aware frictional numerical formulations. These facts establish material and contact precedents; they do not demonstrate active transformation or dominant loss in MP1-R bending.

**Comparison across studies.** S05 tests nearly isothermal hierarchical cables and components in tension, resolving local fields beyond the global curve. S11 tests mixed NiTiNOL/steel ropes in cyclic bending and dynamics, whereas S13 tests braided microfilaments using small-amplitude DMA/thermal and tensile measurements. Their material architecture, loading, temperature paths and measured losses differ. **Mechanistic connections.** CROSS-STUDY INFERENCE: material state changes the deformation/force carried by wires, which can alter contact loading and slip through ordinary equilibrium and existing constitutive/contact laws. A constant modulus may fail in a transformation-active domain without requiring a novel coupling term. Conversely, friction can contribute to a loop without transformation being active. Conventional transformation-aware mechanics is a live H0b alternative to either simplistic explanation.

**Methodological critique.** Global hysteresis is an aggregate of material, interface, sleeve and fixture effects. S05 offers stronger local material observables than global loops, but its nearly isothermal tension domain cannot be imported as a universal bending threshold or as evidence for MP1-R phase activity. S11 dynamic guide friction and S13 small-amplitude/frequency-dependent measures prevent quantitative loss pooling. Declaring either material or friction dominant from this corpus would exceed the observations.

**MP1-R relevance.** A same-lot material baseline and structural-wire temperature/strain/history are needed if material mechanism or H0a sufficiency is claimed. Transformation-sensitive characterization should match the accessible MP1-R domain rather than assume NiTi identity guarantees activity. Existing numerical material/contact formulations set a demanding conventional alternative.

**Remaining uncertainty.** No validated source independently partitions transformation and friction in the proposed pressure-controlled bending assembly. The extent to which either contributes, and whether state-conditioned pressure effects require a term beyond an appropriate conventional model, remain unresolved.

### T05 — Integration evidence and competing explanations

Evidence boundary: S03, S04, S05, S06, S09, S11, S12, S13, S18, S22, V-W02; ANALOGUE_ONLY; MECHANISTIC_ANALOGUE; P411, P412, P413, P414, P417, P418, P419, P420. Phase 3 lineage: C011, C013, C018, C019, C020, C021. Axes: 2, 3, 5, 6, 7.

**Comparison across studies.** S03/S04 are substantial partial prior art for pressure/friction and SMA/friction/bending, respectively. **Important contrasts.** H0a-R asks whether a calibrated material-dominated explanation suffices; it is broader than constant-E substitution. **Mechanistic connections.** CROSS-STUDY INFERENCE: a staged inference can first establish an empirical envelope, then constrain pressure transfer and interface/material contributions, then evaluate locked conventional predictions on reserved conditions. **Methodological critique.** A calibrated parameter absorbing unknown pressure transmission or sleeve effects can give good global fits while assigning the wrong mechanism. **MP1-R relevance.** Characterization remains meaningful even if H0b adequately describes the eventual response. Claims should scale to independently supported observables: pressure-conditioned stiffness first, interface or transformation attribution only when supported, and new-law necessity only after stronger discrimination. The synthesis therefore carries unresolved questions and conditional requirements into reconciliation rather than a final gap.

**Remaining uncertainty.** All MP1-R-specific hypothesis adequacy judgments remain prospective. No direct full-chain overlap is identified in the validated evidence base, but the coverage cannot establish worldwide absence. The accessible transformation/contact domain and claim ambition need later adjudication, without treating uncertainty as novelty.
