   Many studies have been conducted to realize stiffness con-
trol. Cianchetti et al. proposed a manipulator for minimal ac-
cess surgery that can implement selective stiffness changes by
combining a fluid actuator to enable omnidirectional bending,
elongation functionality, and jamming [18], [19]. Jiang et al.
proposed a high DOF variable stiffness mechanism that can be
highly refracted. The rigidity of each joint can be independently
controlled using the jamming principle through vacuum gener-
ation, and the effect of vacuum pressure and particle type on
stiffness adjustment can be studied [20]. Kim et al. proposed a
stiffness conversion mechanism layer jamming through friction
between the layers of a snake-shaped hollow manipulator com-
posed of several layers of thin mylar film and tested the stiffness
and fracture strength of the layer jamming [21], [22]. Moses et al.
[23] proposed a stiffness control manipulator whose main body
includes thirty interlocking fibers and a flexible sheath around
the manipulator. The fibers are free to slide along the flexible
sheath that may be evacuated to increase its stiffness. Although
granular and layer jamming approaches achieve significant stiff-
ness, the system still requires a large volume or layered structure
