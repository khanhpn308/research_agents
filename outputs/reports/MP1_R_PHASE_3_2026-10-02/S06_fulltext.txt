A new mechanical model of short wire ropes: Theory and experimental
validation
F. Barsi ∗, B. Carboni, W. Lacarbonara
Department of Structural and Geotechnical Engineering, Sapienza University of Rome, Via Eudossiana 18, Rome, 00184, Italy



ARTICLE               INFO                               ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                A new mechanical formulation is presented for assembling the constitutive relationships of single- and multiple-
Wire ropes                                               strand wire ropes. The model aims to predict the lower and upper bounds of the bending stiffness associated to
Shear deformable beam                                    the limit behaviors of perfect stick and full slip between the constituting wires. The wire rope is described as
Eigenstrains
                                                         a shear deformable beam for which the generalized constitutive equations, accounting for the wires’ geometry
Curved thin rod
                                                         and material properties, are derived according to Saint-Venant’s theory extended to curvilinear thin rods. The
Stick–slip
Hysteresis cycles
                                                         distinguished feature of wire ropes, i.e., the possibility of relative sliding between the wires, is accounted for by
                                                         the introduction of eigenstrains in the wires’ strain measures. An experimental campaign is carried out on two
                                                         series of short steel wire ropes subjected to quasi-static bending loops obtaining hysteresis cycles for different
                                                         amplitudes and rope lengths. The model is validated by comparing theoretically predicted and experimentally
                                                         measured stiffnesses obtained from the bending hysteresis cycles as well as from axial-torsional tests present
                                                         in the literature.
