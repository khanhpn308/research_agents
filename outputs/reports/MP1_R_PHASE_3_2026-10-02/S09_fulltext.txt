                        Actuated Robotic Pump
                                             Matthew D. Pierce and Stephen A. Mascaro, Member, IEEE


        Abstract—This paper presents the concept, design, optimiza-
     tion, and experimental analysis of a biologically inspired wet shape
     memory alloy (SMA) actuated pump for robotic and mechatronic
     systems. Just as the human heart provides energy to the muscles in
     the body, the robotic SMA pump distributes thermofluidic energy
     to arrays of SMA actuators that function as robotic muscles. Fur-
     thermore, the robotic pump draws from its own fluidic output to
     assist in the actuation of its own internal SMA actuators, just as a
     portion of the blood pumped by the human heart supplies energy

                                                                                        Future improvements planned for the robotic pump include
                                                                                     making the system more compact in volume, removing the need
                                                                                     for auxiliary electric inputs, and optimizing the practical effi-
                                                                                     ciency of the pump. Making the system more compact while
                                                                                     maintaining a comparable volumetric output would require a
                                                                                     creative use of space while taking care not to impair the con-
                                                                                     traction of the SMA actuators. Methods to decrease the electrical
                                                                                     dependence include using fluid-only heating and using mechan-
                                                                                     ically timed valves.
                                                                                        As mentioned previously, the maximum theoretical efficiency
                                                                                     that this system can achieve is 3.4%. The actual efficiency of this
                                                                                     prototype system is much less, but the goal at this stage was not
                                                                                     to optimize efficiency but rather to first demonstrate that a net
   Fig. 12. Experimental performance with fluid-only actuation and 40-cm ac-         positive volumetric output was practically achievable. In future
   tuators, with and without heat input to the hot accumulator water.                work, we will seek to characterize and optimize the practical
                                                                                     efficiency of the system by minimizing thermal and mechanical
                                                                                     losses. The efficiency of the optimized system will still be much
                                                                                     lower than a pump driven by electric motors. However, this
                                                                                     efficiency disadvantage is theoretically offset by a 100 to 1
                                                                                     advantage in chemical versus electric energy storage. The SMA
                                                                                     pump also has the unique advantage of silent operation, which
                                                                                     may be important for certain applications. In theory, the SMA
                                                                                     pump could operate continuously and silently with only a high-
                                                                                     energy-density chemical energy source to keep the hot fluid hot.
