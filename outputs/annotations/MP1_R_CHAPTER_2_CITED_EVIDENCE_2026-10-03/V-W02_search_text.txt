B4: ABSTRACT
In this paper, based on three-dimensional phenomenological model and using a user-defined
material subroutine mechanical behavior of shape memory alloy (SMA) wire ropes (or cables) and
their components have been studied through implicit solution method in Abaqus software.
Material parameters have been extracted using available experimental data and numerical simula-
tions. Due to the convoluted geometry and interwire contact status within a cable, a finite elem-
ent analysis is firstly performed for a 1  37 steel wire rope to validate modeling and mechanical
interactions of a wire rope. Afterwards, superelastic and shape memory effect cables with different
constructions (7 7 and 1  27) are studied and their mechanical behavior including normal stress,
shear stress, strain, and temperature diagrams as well as the relationship between axial force and
displacement for a full cable and each component are extracted. Furthermore, the SMA wire ropes
have been optimized in terms of achieving maximum specific energy through experimental design
method. Comparison of numerical results with experimental data indicates the acceptable
accuracy of this study.

B7: 1. Introduction
B8: Applications of wire ropes are widely increased due to their
significant flexibility in torsional and bending purposes com-
pared to solid bars at equal overall cross-sectional area.
They are one of the most important means of lifting, hoist-
ing and transmission of mechanical power. Towing, main-
tenance of suspension bridges, vessel mooring, and oil
platforms are also among the most important usage of
cables [1]. Despite failure of one or several wires, cable can
provide higher safety when compared with monolithic bars
so that they are more likely to incur high potential applica-
tions of these tools in several engineering fields. Their vari-
ous

a

different mechanical properties in terms of flexibility, abra-
sion resistance, and significant corrosion resistance [2].
B9: To date, several works have studied the mechanical
behavior of conventional cables for different loads and cross
sections [2–6]. Under torsional and axial loadings, theory of
conventional cables and wire interactions are investigated
[2]. Equilibrium equations of a 1  7 steel cable under ten-
sile force are derived and compared with experimental data
[3]. Modeling and simulation of steel wires as well as 1  37
and double-helix wire rope are performed by Stanova et al.
[4, 5]. Moreover, the simulation of a steel cable with con-
struction of 19  6 has been studied considering the elasto-
plastic material properties [6]. In cables under torsional
and/or axial loads, with a sudden decrease in load, each wire
may separate from the core wire and consequently, the cable
B10: becomes permanently deformed (bird cage phenomenon)
[2]. In order to incur such defects and improve the perform-
ance of wire ropes, the use of shape memory alloy (SMA)
cables has widely received a great attention. Due to their
unique characteristics including superelasticity, shape mem-
ory effect, high energy absorption, adequate tensile strength,
high corrosion resistance, and the ability to recover large
strains, these cables are resistant to twisting and impact
loads [7]. In contrast to other conventional cables, SMA
wire ropes owing to their high energy density, can provide a
design with the higher power but the lower volume [1, 7].
Shape memory effect structures (SME) can be act as a ther-
mal lock or thermal actuator due to the detwinning process
[8–15]. Large-load capacity and high impact energy absorp-
tion can be considered as an inherit characterization of these
cables [1]. The use of superelastic (SE) cables may be also
common in dentistry [7], relieving congestion [16], cellular
phone antenna [17], and vibration alleviation of earthquakes
[18]. Several researches explore mechanical properties of SE
wire ropes [19–23]. An analytical, numerical and experimen-
tal study of SE coil spring under axial loading is performed
using three-dimensional model [19]. Mechanical response of
a set of SE wire ropes with two constructions of 7  7 and
1  27 under tensile stress is investigated experimentally [1,
7]. Mas et al. [20] explored the performance of Ni-Ti SMA
cable in reinforced concrete application and its bond proper-
ties. Cyclic uniaxial tests are conducted by Ozbulute and
Sherif [21, 22] to investigate its potential application under
fatigue behavior. In civil engineering, several studied are

B17: 4. Simulation of steel and SMA wire ropes
B18: In this section, to validate the process of modeling and

1  37 in the elastic range is analyzed using finite element

B3: 7. Summary and conclusions
B4: In the present paper, a detailed study of the mechanical
behavior of two configurations of single- and double-helix
SMA cables under two characteristics of SE and SME is dis-
cussed. For validation of geometry and modeling of the
cables, FEA of 1  37 steel wire rope is achieved and then
compared with existing numerical results. Extracting mater-
ial parameters from the experimental study of 1  27 and
7  7 SE wire ropes under tensile load, the normal and shear
stress-strain
for
full
cables
and
their
components
are
B5: obtained through FEA and then compared with experimen-
tal data. Furthermore, single- and double-helix SME cables
are simulated and the relationship between of both normal
and shear stresses with strain and temperature variation
along with force-displacement diagrams for components are
investigated. Moreover, optimization of the 1  27 SME
cable to fulfill the maximum specific energy is carried out in
terms of two controllable variables including wire diameter
and helix angle and then the optimized corresponding val-
ues are suggested. The force-displacement and normal
stress-strain diagrams show that although the axial force in
core wires seems to be relatively insignificant, due to small
cross-sectional area these wires have always the largest nor-
mal stress. From inner layers to outers, the normal stress
starts decreasing while the axial force does not follow a spe-
cific trend so that for different elongations different cable
components could have the highest axial force. In the shear
stress-strain-temperature diagram of SME cables, lays of
individual components contribute to positive and/or nega-
tive sign of shear stress. Finally, upon heating these cables,
they recover inelastic strains in very short range of time and
specific range of temperature. The results of optimization
also shows that the more wire diameter and the less helix
angle can lead to the greater specific energy of a wire rope.
