B7: A B S T R A C T
B8: NiTi alloys exhibit high damping capacity and superelasticity. However, their internal friction peak typically 
occurs near the martensitic transformation temperature, limiting their applications over a wide temperature 
window as damping devices. Herein, with NiTi microfilaments introducing sequential localized phase trans­
formations, structural deformation and inter-wire friction dissipation, the periodic braided microfilament 
transformed the internal friction peak into a high broad damping plateau with a reduced modulus. Notably, the 
88B microfilament exhibited a high tanδ (~0.11) and low storage modulus (~3.6 GPa) across a wide temper­
ature window exceeding 145 ◦C. This work realizes the synergistic optimization of high damping, ultra-low 
stiffness, and temperature insensitivity in NiTi wires, offering a promising strategy for designing high- 
performance damping devices under complex environments.
B9: 1. Introduction
B10: NiTi shape memory alloys are widely utilized in aerospace and pre­
cision instruments due to their high damping capacity,[1–3] which pri­
marily originates from reversible martensitic transformation (MT) and 
the motion of abundant movable twin boundaries.[4,5] However, the 
damping peak is typically restricted to a narrow transformation tem­
perature range, exhibiting temperature dependence.[6] In the fully 
austenitic or martensitic phase, the absence of mobile phase interfaces 
reduces damping capacity, with a substantial contrast in loss tangent 
(tanδ) between the two phases.[7] To broaden damping performance in a 
wide temperature window, reduce temperature sensitivity, and improve 
modulus stability, second phases have been introduced to induce local 
transformation stress fields.[8] Alternatively, structural designs such as 
porous NiTi and bicontinuous interpenetrating Mg-NiTi composites can 
simultaneously enhance strength and damping capacity.[9] In this study, 
we developed novel NiTi braided microfilaments featuring a spatial 
helical architecture to achieve enhanced wide-temperature damping 
and a tunable elastic modulus. The spatial interlacing and bending of 
wires in the braided architecture create spring-like mechanical units that 
B11: synergistically couple intrinsic phase transformation of NiTi with fric­
tional energy dissipation from inter-wire contact and micro-sliding 
during vibration.
B12: 2. Experimental
B13: The material information and braiding process details have been 
described in detail in our previous paper.[10] Single-braided microfila­
ments were fabricated using 4, 8, and 12 NiTi wires on a high-speed 
braiding machine, denoted as 4B, 8B, and 12B, respectively, with a 
braiding density parameter (picks per inch, ppi) set to 50. Double- 
braided microfilaments (denoted as 88B) were prepared by re-braiding 
eight 8B microfilaments using an identical manufacturing process. 8 
wires were also prepared with ppi values of 25 and 80, denoted as ppi25 
and ppi80, respectively. The damping capacity and storage modulus 
were studied with a TA Q850 dynamic mechanical analyzer (DMA) at a 
fixed strain amplitude of 20 μm at different frequencies and a cooling 
and heating rate of 5 ◦C/min. The tests were performed over a broad 
temperature window ranging from −70 ◦C to 75 ◦C.

B2: Fig. 2a and b exhibit morphologies of 8B-NiTi and 88B-NiTi micro­
filaments. In contrast to the single wire (Fig. 1), the storage modulus 
decreases significantly after single braiding (Fig. 2d) and further de­
creases following the double-braid process (Fig. 2e). Specifically, the 
storage modulus of 8B-NiTi is at approximately 11 GPa during cooling 
process and 12 GPa during heating process, which further decreases to 
~3.6 GPa and ~4.3 GPa in 88B-NiTi. Furthermore, over the tested 
temperature range of 145 ◦C, the tanδ of the braided microfilaments 
remains temperature-insensitive, transitioning into a high broad inter­
nal friction plateau that demonstrates superior thermomechanical sta­
bility. Throughout the temperature range, the tanδ of 8B-NiTi remains 
stable at 0.02–0.05, exhibiting a significantly improved damping ca­
pacity in the austenite phase compared with the single wire. Notably, 
Fig. 2f demonstrates that the tanδ curves of 8B are nearly identical after 
10 thermal cycles, evidencing the excellent cycling stability of the 
damping mechanism. Cyclic tensile tests were further conducted on 
braided microfilaments, as shown in Fig. S1. Upon further double- 
braiding process, the 88B microfilaments achieve a higher tanδ of 
0.06–0.11 over a wide temperature window. Notably, the tanδ and 
modulus curves at 1 Hz and 5 Hz nearly overlap, revealing excellent 
frequency stability and reliable damping under variable conditions. 
Moreover，we compared the damping capacity of stainless steel wire, 
stainless steel braided microfilament, and twisted stainless steel wire, as 
shown in Fig. S2.
B3: Within 8B, individual NiTi wires follow a helical path (Fig. 2c), 
forming a continuous micro-spring network that converts structural 
deformation into localized bending and torsion. This inhomogeneous 
stress distribution within braided structure induces sequential localized 
MT,[10] broadening the intrinsic damping peak into a wide plateau. 88B 
microfilament creates a micro-spring network that dissipates vibration 
energy progressively from outer to inner architectures,[11] further 
enhancing damping performance. Simultaneously, abundant contact 
sites generate micro-sliding friction interfaces, enabling efficient energy 
dissipation.
B4: As shown in Fig. 3a and b, increasing the number of wires increases 
the density of inter-wire contact sites. The tanδ of 4B is smooth and 
maintains a lower value of 0.008–0.02 (Fig. 3c), and 12B achieves a 
higher tanδ value of 0.03–0.06 (Fig. 3d). With the ppi maintained at 50, 
all samples (4B, 8B and 12B) show a broad damping plateau without 
distinct internal friction peaks. Overall, damping capacity improves 
with the increasing number of wires. The storage modulus of 4B remains 
around 17–19 GPa, and 12B reaches approximately 18–22 GPa. As the 
wire increases from 4B to 8B and 12B, exhibits no distinct abrupt 
change, displaying temperature independence. Increasing the wire 
count raises the wire volume fraction, synchronously increasing the 
interwoven micro-springs and contact sites, thereby simultaneously 
enhancing structural damping and frictional dissipation via micro- 
sliding.
B5: In Fig. 4a and b, increasing the ppi produces a significantly denser 
braided architecture, directly reflecting a larger braiding angle. At low 
density (ppi25), the storage modulus and internal friction retain 
intrinsic phase transformation features (Fig. 4c). During the cooling 
process, tanδ exhibits a damping peak near −20 ◦C with a maximum 
value of ~0.10, similar to the behavior of the single NiTi wire. This 
indicates that at a low braiding density, the structural constraint 
imposed by braiding is relatively weak. For ppi80 (Fig. 4d), tanδ be­
comes flat in the temperature window, remaining in the low range of 
0.02–0.04. In single-braided system, incorporating the result for 8B- 
ppi50 reveals that increasing the braiding density from ppi25 to ppi50 
and ppi80 leads to the gradual disappearance of damping peaks and 
modulus steps, achieving temperature-independent behavior alongside 
a decrease in modulus and tanδ. The transition from the non-linear 
phase transformation behavior at low braiding density to damping 
plateau at higher densities is consistent with the strain hardening-like 
effects arising from the increased geometric constraints.The ppi25 fea­
tures a long braid pitch and sparse contact crossovers, allowing high 
B6: deformation for individual wires. In contrast, ppi80 exhibits a larger 
braiding angle, forming tightly intertwined wires with high crossover 
density. This geometric constraint restricts the axial extension and 
bending of the NiTi wires, shortening the micro-slip displacement at the 
contact sites. Consequently, despite more contact sites, the limited 
deformation displacement reduces friction energy dissipation and at­
tenuates overall damping capacity. Additionally, we compared the 
damping performance of metallic structural materials, as shown in 
Table S1.
B7: 4. Conclusion
B8: In summary, braided NiTi microfilaments couple intrinsic MT with 
structural energy dissipation to achieve a flat damping plateau and 
reduced stiffness over a wide temperature window (>145 ◦C). Spatial 
constraints introduce inhomogeneity stress, enabling a sequential MT 
that converts the damping peak of a single wire into a high-damping 
plateau. Braided wires act as micro-springs that transform vibration 
into individual wire bending and torsional strains, driving inter-wire 
micro-sliding friction. From single to double braiding, modulus de­
creases while damping improves. Increasing wire count improves 
damping capacity, whereas increasing ppi reduces both damping ca­
pacity and storage modulus. Braided microfilaments overcome the 
narrow damping window and high modulus of conventional NiTi wires, 
providing a strategy for high-performance damping devices in complex 
thermomechanical environments.
