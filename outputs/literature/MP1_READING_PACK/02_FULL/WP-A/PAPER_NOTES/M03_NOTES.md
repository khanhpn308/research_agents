## A. Research problem and objectives
Russo et al. (2023) deliver an exhaustive, modern cross-disciplinary overview of continuum robots, synthesizing advances across design, mechanics modeling, sensing, and control. The core research challenge addressed is how to accurately represent, predict, and control the non-linear continuous bending behavior of slender, flexible robotic bodies subjected to internal actuation forces and external environmental interactions ([M03-E01](file:///home/khanh/projects/mechanical-research-agents/outputs/literature/MP1_READING_PACK/02_FULL/WP-A/EVIDENCE_RECORDS.jsonl), p. 1). The paper aims to bridge mechanical engineering, robotics, and applied physics by categorizing modeling methods from fast kinematics to high-fidelity non-linear continuum mechanics.

## B. Physical system, materials, geometry and topology
Continuum robots are physically defined as slender robotic structures with a high length-to-diameter ratio ($L/D \gg 1$), featuring a compliant backbone capable of continuous bending along its entire length ([M03-E01](file:///home/khanh/projects/mechanical-research-agents/outputs/literature/MP1_READING_PACK/02_FULL/WP-A/EVIDENCE_RECORDS.jsonl), p. 1). The authors categorize modern architectures into:
1. **Tendon-driven continuum robots:** Rigid disks (vertebrae) spaced along flexible elastic rods or superelastic NiTi lamellae, guiding cables that pull the tip or intermediate segments (Fig. 1, p. 3).
2. **Concentric tube continuum robots (CTCR):** Nested, pre-curved, elastic NiTi tubes translated and rotated relative to each other (Fig. 2a, b, p. 4).
3. **Rod-driven continuum robots:** Parallel elastic push-pull rods distributed around the periphery (Fig. 2c, d, p. 4).
4. **Fluidic soft robots:** Hyper-elastic silicone/elastomer bodies actuated by pressurized chambers.

## C. Physical mechanism and loading
The continuous deformation of the backbone curve is driven by bending moments, axial compression, and shear forces generated by internal or external actuators. The paper highlights the fundamental coupling between actuation routing and structural deformation:
- In tendon-driven systems, pulling tendons induces distributed normal forces against spacer disks, generating discrete moment couples that approximate continuous bending. High tendon tension simultaneously induces large compressive preloads that can trigger structural instability or lateral buckling.
- In concentric tube robots, mutual elastic contact couples bending and torsional moments across tube walls.
- Structural compliance permits passive adaptation to obstacles, but causes significant deflection under unmodeled environmental contact forces or payload.

## D. Mechanical model, equations, assumptions and boundary conditions
Section 3 and Table 1 ([M03-E02](file:///home/khanh/projects/mechanical-research-agents/outputs/literature/MP1_READING_PACK/02_FULL/WP-A/EVIDENCE_RECORDS.jsonl), p. 3 [printed pp. 2–3]) chart the full spectrum of continuum robot modeling methodologies:
1. **Geometric Constant-Curvature Models (PCC):** Assume piecewise constant curvature circular arcs. Extremely computationally efficient; suitable for real-time kinematic control, but neglects external loads, gravity, friction, and non-uniform cross-sections.
2. **Pseudo-Rigid-Body (PRB) Models:** Approximate the flexible backbone as an articulated chain of rigid links connected by virtual torsional springs. Bridges rigid-robotics algorithms and flexible structures.
3. **Modal Reduced-Order Models (ROM):** Formulate backbone deformation using a set of assumed spatial basis functions (e.g. Chebyshev polynomials, modal shapes), projecting continuous PDEs into finite-dimensional ODE state-space systems.
4. **Continuum Mechanics Cosserat & Kirchhoff Rod Models:** Geometrically exact formulations based on non-linear rod mechanics. The Cosserat rod model represents the backbone as a directed space curve with attached orthonormal reference frames, capturing tension, shear, bending, and torsion via coupled non-linear ODE boundary value problems (BVPs).
5. **Non-linear Finite Element Methods (FEM):** 3D continuum discretization capable of capturing complex cross-sectional distortion and hyper-elastic material behavior, but computationally prohibitive for real-time control.

## E. Experimental setup and measured quantities
The paper surveys experimental validation protocols across hundreds of literature studies:
- Optical motion capture (e.g. Vicon, Qualisys), electromagnetic tracking, and endoscopic camera image processing to capture 3D backbone curvature and tip trajectory.
- Multi-axis load cells and fiber Bragg grating (FBG) optical strain sensors integrated into the backbone for distributed shape and contact force reconstruction.
- Measured physical quantities include actuator displacement, cable tension, tip position error, and lateral payload force.

## F. Quantitative findings
The review evaluates the trade-off frontier between computational speed and predictive accuracy:
- PCC kinematics solves within microseconds but exhibits large errors ($> 10\text{–}20\%$ of robot length) when subjected to external tip loads.
- Cosserat rod kinetostatics achieves high accuracy (tip errors $< 2\text{–}5\%$) at execution frequencies of $50\text{–}500\text{ Hz}$.
- 3D FEM achieves highest fidelity but execution rates remain orders of magnitude below real-time control requirements.

## I. Author limitations and counterevidence
Crucially, Section 1.2 and Section 3.3 explicitly identify unresolved, open mechanics and control challenges ([M03-E03](file:///home/khanh/projects/mechanical-research-agents/outputs/literature/MP1_READING_PACK/02_FULL/WP-A/EVIDENCE_RECORDS.jsonl), p. 4):
1. **Friction Modeling Along Flexible Channels:** Tendon-vertebra friction creates path-dependent tension losses and stick-slip hysteresis that standard rod models do not accurately capture.
2. **Cross-Sectional Distortion and Material Non-linearity:** Slender flexible structures deform non-uniformly across their cross-sections under bending, violating classical planar assumptions.
3. **Bifurcation and Buckling Instabilities:** Concentric tubes and tendon-loaded rods undergo structural snapping and torsional buckling at critical configurations.
4. **Generalization Limits of Model-Less Approaches:** Data-driven and neural network controllers fail dramatically when encountering configurations or loads outside their calibration datasets.
