## A. Research problem and objectives
The objective of Feak & Swales (2009) is pedagogical and methodological: guiding graduate students and early-career researchers in transforming a literature review from a superficial, disjointed list of paper summaries into an organized, persuasive "research story" ([M01-E01](file:///home/khanh/projects/mechanical-research-agents/outputs/literature/MP1_READING_PACK/02_FULL/WP-A/EVIDENCE_RECORDS.jsonl), p. 3). The book addresses how to establish a scholarly research territory, identify specific niches or gaps in prior knowledge, and explicitly justify the necessity of the proposed research questions. It is critical to distinguish this methodological writing guidance from primary mechanical evidence: M01 establishes how to structure claims about mechanics literature, but contains no physical mechanics findings itself.

## I. Author limitations and counterevidence
Feak & Swales explicitly acknowledge that literature reviews in STEM fields often present unique writing challenges: technical terminology may limit opportunities for paraphrasing, and graduate students often struggle between uncritically accepting prior publications and prematurely claiming revolutionary novelty. A well-constructed review must report previous work fairly, identifying exact boundary conditions under which prior results hold, rather than dismissing prior studies through exaggerated claims of limitations.
